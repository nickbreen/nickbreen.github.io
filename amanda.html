My Philosophy

I believe children lean through doing and getting involved while taking age-appropriate risks.

I believe following the children’s interests by using a range of activities to extend their learning will create confident learners.

I believe independence is a really important part of childcare, and I encourage as much independence for their age and ability.

I aim to plan and create a positive learning environment for all children and foster their learning, so they can form positive meaningful relationships with their peers through play.

I value the relationships I form with children and whanau to create a positive environment for learning.

I value getting out into the community and utilizing parks, bushes and beaches that we have so close to us.

Transitioning Into Home-Based Care

Welcome to Amanda’s home-based care with Tiny Nation.

Thank you for choosing me to be your child’s educator. I am really looking forward to getting to know your child and whanau to settle your child into care.

As part of this transitioning process here are some tips on what can help with the settling process.

While all Tamariki and Whānau are individuals and have different needs, I have found in my experience that this is the best way forward to managing these tricky and emotional transitions. I understand that it is hard for both children and their parents to let go, and move onto the next stage, so my role is to make this as positive and stress-free as possible.

Parent and child visits

I recommend 2-3 visits with a parent or whānau member so that your child can see that it is a safe place and become familiar with both me and other children and the environment. Seeing that their parents are happy and that this is a positive experience helps when the time comes to leaving your child. This allows me to begin developing a relationship with your child which is key to a successful transition.

These visits are scheduled for 1 hour and at a time that suits both me and your child. There is no charge for these 3 visits.

Drop Off Visits

The next stage of the process is to leave your child for short visits up to 1 hour to begin with, then extend these out until your child is ready to stay for a full day.

These are to be booked in on your child’s scheduled days.

You must have filled out all the enrolment details and have a confirmed official start date. Your child will need to be considered part of the ratio for these visits, so my time is charged at my hourly rate. I will invoice you for these visits.

For these visits, it is best to drop and go and return within the agreed time frame. If your child is happy upon pick up that is great and your child will be left knowing Mum and Dad always come back, and it is a positive experience. Staying for a long time and delaying the inevitable can prolong any stress to both children and parents.

Transition Visits

The best time to visit is when your child has had food, are changed and has slept well. Either in the morning or after nap times.

If your child has a dummy or comforter, I recommend bringing these to help with transitioning.

Talk about the visits and how fun it will be with your child. Keep it positive and upbeat. Try not to be nervous or worried as children can pick up adult's emotions.

Communicate with me about your child’s needs, sleep and be honest about how things are — there is no judgement if you have had a hard day — I have been there!

Share your child’s interests with me so I can make the learning environment fun and engaging for them.

Before starting home-based care, it is always helpful to get your child into a good sleep routine, encouraging them to self-settle.
